## Technical summary

The repository and deployment evidence identify **21 external provider or managed-platform records**. All 21 are disposed through the four keyed matrices below, and every record explicitly answers every field required by the audit brief. A packaged adapter is not proof of an active external account or a working production connection.

At the final checkpoint, repository HEAD is `ff3c8ef`, the application source remains `4a5cd19`, and production remains Sites v199. The audit read the names of Sites environment entries at revision 18 without reading, copying, or testing any secret value. Environment key presence proves only that a named entry exists. A key not listed proves only that the expected name was not exposed in that environment snapshot. In both cases, the external account, credential validity, credential value, account owner, billing, quota, support plan, and runtime health remain **UNKNOWN** unless stated otherwise.

The strongest integration blocker is the Sites policy boundary. The application contains payment-enablement flows. Section 2.5 of the current ChatGPT Sites Terms prohibits a Site from initiating, executing, or otherwise facilitating financial transactions, and section 3.3 prohibits payment-card data. Provider-hosted checkout reduces direct card-data handling, but the exact interpretation of an external checkout redirect under the broad facilitation wording requires written OpenAI confirmation. Until that confirmation exists, payment enablement on Sites is a blocking policy risk rather than an accepted production capability.

No live payment, invoice export, calendar write, message, lead submission, address lookup, Firebase administrative action, mobile push, storage recovery, or provider-console action was performed. The matrices therefore separate source behavior, environment-key observations, and external operational truth.

## Sites payment-policy and data boundary

Two official sources govern the present assessment:

- [ChatGPT Sites Terms, updated 2026-07-09](https://openai.com/policies/chatgpt-sites-terms/): section 2.5 prohibits Sites from initiating, executing, or otherwise facilitating money, cryptocurrency, or other financial/investment transactions. Section 3.3 prohibits Sites from handling PHI or PCI-DSS payment-card data and places Hosted Data controller obligations on the Site operator.
- [Creating and managing ChatGPT Sites](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001339-creating-and-managing-chatgpt-sites): documents the beta/plan controls, production-only deployment URLs, managed D1/R2 storage, permanent Site deletion behavior, and lack of data/inference residency support at launch.

Observed code uses provider-hosted Stripe/Square checkout and does not store raw card details in the reviewed ledgers. That narrows the payment-card-data finding but does not resolve the broader transaction-facilitation wording. The correct disposition is:

1. Direct card-data processing on Sites is prohibited and is not an acceptable design.
2. The implemented payment-enablement routes create a direct policy conflict if enabled within Sites as prohibited facilitation.
3. Whether an external hosted-checkout redirect is permitted under the exact Sites contract requires written OpenAI/provider confirmation. Absence of card data is not sufficient evidence.
4. Until confirmed, disable or relocate membership/payment transaction enablement to an approved host. Preserve TLink's integer-cent authoritative invoice and reconciliation ledgers.

## Manual recovery order by integration class

1. **Financial and accounting providers:** disable new actions, preserve TLink ledger state, inspect the provider by external/request/event ID, reconcile amount/currency/status, and never create a second payment or invoice while the first outcome is unknown.
2. **OAuth providers:** preserve encrypted connection metadata, record the exact failure stage, reconnect only for an actual authorization failure, and validate least-privilege scopes after reconnection.
3. **Signed webhooks:** retain bounded event/correlation identifiers and verification result without secrets, use D1 uniqueness, and replay only through a controlled provider-supported mechanism.
4. **Messaging:** reconcile by provider message ID, respect durable opt-outs and resend caps, keep ambiguous attempts out of delivered state, and avoid minting a new secure link solely because callback status is delayed.
5. **Calendars:** keep TLink authoritative, retry explicit sync, recreate a missing mapped event only after a provider 404, and never import external edits as truth.
6. **Lead relay:** inspect deployment version, trigger history, Sheet dedupe, ACL, and probe result before replaying by lead reference. The Google dependency cannot be its own sole recovery channel.
7. **Read-only data sources:** expose partial/stale provenance, retain last-good data where implemented, verify source integrity/format, and never relabel a fallback estimate as current official data.
8. **Mobile:** revoke compromised devices, abort their multipart sessions, clear push tokens, restart from a safe sync cursor, and resolve revision conflicts against authoritative TLink state.
